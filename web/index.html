Masking Curve Lab

Measure your own frequency-masking curves with headphones.

Uncalibrated system. All levels are in dBFS (decibels relative to digital full scale), not dB SPL. The real loudness at your ear depends on your computer volume and headphones, so only differences between levels (such as the amount of masking) are meaningful. Safety: nothing louder than −10 dBFS is ever played.

Before you start
  1. Use headphones, not laptop speakers. Turn off spatial audio, EQ and other sound "enhancements".
  2. Quiet room. Close apps that make sounds and silence notifications.
  3. Set the volume once. Start with your computer volume low, press Comfort check, and raise the volume until the masker is clearly audible but comfortable. Then press Loudest-sound check — it must never feel unpleasant.
  4. Don't change the system volume during the experiment, or your thresholds will shift.
  5. Take a break every ~10 minutes. The app reminds you; use Pause.
  6. Answer honestly. In yes/no mode some trials contain no probe (catch trials). In two-interval mode, guess if unsure.
  7. Suggested order: measure the baseline (masker off) first, then the masked curve for each masker frequency.

    How the staircases work
    • Probe starts at −20 dBFS (clearly audible). Level range −100 to −10 dBFS.
    • B · Yes/No: heard → probe 1 step softer, not heard → 1 step louder (1-up/1-down, converges on the ~50 % "heard" point). About 10 % of trials are catch trials (masker only, no probe); they don't move the staircase but false "heard" answers are logged.
    • C · 2IFC: two intervals 400 ms apart; one has the probe. Two correct in a row → softer, one wrong → louder (2-down/1-up, ~70.7 % correct).
    • Step 6 dB until 2 reversals, then 2 dB. Stops after 8 reversals; threshold = mean of the last 6 reversal levels. Safety stop after 100 trials (marked "did not converge").
    • Timing: masker 500 ms; probe 300 ms, centred inside the masker; 20 ms raised-cosine ramps on every sound.
